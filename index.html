What I do

I give people
a reliable way
to succeed together.

Most hospitality businesses are good at opening. The concept, the launch, the excitement of something new. The harder part is making it work — consistently, profitably, without the founder in every room.

I notice where everyday work takes more effort than it should. Information entered twice. Approvals chased. A single person holding the knowledge that should be shared. Those details tell you where clearer responsibilities or simpler processes could help people do their jobs better — and reduce cost.

Whether a business is growing, struggling, or making its first move into a new market, the work is the same: understand what is already there, agree what needs attention first, and build something the team can carry forward without depending on me.

On menus
If your team has to explain the whole menu before a guest can order, adding another dish may be solving the wrong problem. Every dish is a promise the next shift must keep — and when those promises compete for limited prep space and attention, service slows and the business carries the cost.
On restructures
Before we change who reports to whom, I want to understand what your managers spend their time chasing. Removing a role might reduce payroll — but if its work lands on your GMs, we've simply moved the cost into overtime, missed checks and less time with guests.
On expansion
The business can afford another site. But can the operation carry it? Funding secures the location and fit-out. It doesn't create experienced managers or additional leadership attention. The real test is whether you can open the next site without destabilising the ones you have.

How I think
"Let operational proof shape what you promise."
A successful tasting proves the product can work. It doesn't prove the operation can repeat it profitably. Brand investment has a role early on — but fixing the identity too soon can lock you into dishes, service promises and packaging that the operation cannot reliably support. The hidden cost is paying twice: first to launch the promise, then to correct it. Before committing more to brand presentation, I want to see the offer run with different shifts, without founder intervention, with preparation time, waste and labour tracked alongside ingredient cost.
"A founder can be too accessible for the structure they are trying to build."
When team members bypass their manager and take every issue directly to the founder, the manager's decision becomes provisional. Authority weakens. Decisions slow down. People learn to escalate upwards rather than solve problems where they happen. The aim is not to make founders distant. It is to make leadership scalable.
"Every dish must earn its place operationally, not just creatively."
A dish can show a healthy margin on paper and still make the wider operation less profitable. Food cost tells only part of the story. I also look at ingredient overlap, preparation labour, execution consistency across all shifts, training dependency and whether customer demand actually justifies the complexity. A better menu should make it easier for the team to succeed.
"More people or new technology may be part of the answer — but I start somewhere else."
When a team is under pressure, I start by understanding their priorities, how decisions are made and how work moves between departments. Investment placed where it makes a lasting difference to both the team and the business is more valuable than investment placed where it is most visible.
